Our approach

We start with people, not technology.

Most people don’t sit around saying, “Hmm, we really could use some more AI in this place.”

So we help clients create and shape their own future working habits. In practice, that means trying new ways of working before you commit to them, then speeding up the adoption of the ideas that stick.

Try many. Keep what sticks.

Pilots are cheap when they’re small. Most ideas should be allowed to settle back down. The few that work get our full attention.

  1. Listen

    We sit with your people and map the habits worth changing, before anyone mentions a tool.

  2. Try

    Pilots in weeks, not quarters, with real users on the licences you already pay for.

  3. Measure

    Every pilot ends with evidence you could hand to a board, a governor or an auditor.

  4. Adopt

    Training built on how people actually learn, so the new habit is still there in six months.

Anthropotechnics

Peter Sloterdijk calls humans practising animals. We become who we are through training. A working habit is a practice, and a practice can be designed. That idea sits under everything Potara does.

“I’m excited, because things I could only dream up before are now within reach, thanks to these fantastic tools in our hands.”

Yasin spent eight years as a digital workplace consultant for organisations including the European Central Bank, NatWest and BT. Then he qualified as a secondary maths teacher. Potara is what happens when those two careers share a room.

He describes the job as cybernetic marriage counsellor. People and machines aren’t enemies. They need a better room to talk in, and someone to help them listen.
